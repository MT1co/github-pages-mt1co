Google Analytics

By Matthew Tavares · Dec 24, 2024

In the world of marketing, digital insights are actually rather new. While not the first kid on the block, Google Analytics launched in 2005, which shows that major digital organizations only began seeing the importance of digital insights this millennium.

Google chose to provide a website analytics tool so users could improve their Google Ads conversions: help clients understand where their users came from, how they’re behaving and who’s converting. Those insights help the client grow revenue and invest more in Google Ads. Win–win.

That ethos hasn’t changed much over time.

Google Analytics isn’t the only tool for the job. Microsoft Clarity, HubSpot and Adobe Analytics, to name a few, fulfil a similar role.

It’s important to become familiar with this data and how to use it. In my consulting experience, especially within non-profit, Marketing Managers often find Google Analytics to be a foreign thing that they don’t want to get into — something the IT team is sort of managing. They’re used to looking strictly at CRM conversion data to improve the program, not user behaviour.

This is the opportunity

These tools show us what users who don’t convert are doing, and that typically represents over 90% of any organization’s traffic. Imagine accessing 90% more data to help optimize a program.

  • Would knowing that 50% of non-converting users land on a specific page and leave within 10 seconds point to bad traffic, or an error on the page?
  • If 50% of users who enter the donation funnel don’t complete their donation, shouldn’t that be looked into?

We can think of hundreds of other scenarios where focusing on users who don’t convert can help optimize our program and improve our audience or funnel strategy.

So get into your Google Analytics today!
