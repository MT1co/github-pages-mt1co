Your Audience

By Matthew Tavares · Nov 8, 2024

Your audience is just as important as your funnel, because it’s the audience that determines the content. Are you posting an online ad? What image and message does the ad have? Do we have content on our site that is relevant to the user? What do we say in our tax receipt email?

The number of audience groups can be infinite, but likely any organization only needs a few. Historically, charities tend to think transactionally when it comes to audience groups, for example:

  • Current donors
  • Active donors
  • Lapsed donors

These segments do have value, but not in helping to develop meaningful content. Saying “I haven’t heard from you in a while” to a lapsed donor, for instance, is not meaningful.

Define audiences the way they see themselves

What can be meaningful is defining an audience the way they see themselves, then speaking to them as individuals.

A cancer charity, for example, likely has a breast cancer audience. We can identify these people by their behaviour. Perhaps they researched a breast cancer information page on your site (assuming you have this content!), which lets you remarket to them. Or they liked a friend’s breast-cancer-related post on Facebook, which lets you target them with Facebook ads (assuming you have a breast-cancer-specific ad).

We can only target, build a funnel and measure success once we’ve defined our audiences.

Try this: hold an exercise with your team where you build your five largest audiences, then talk about what their funnel could look like. Read my thoughts on developing a funnel here.
